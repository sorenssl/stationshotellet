Northern Lights in Piteå — When, Where and How to See Them

The photo above was taken from our own street in Öjebyn. Here is how to improve your chances.

Home › Northern lights in Piteå

Piteå lies at about 65° north, a little over 130 km south of the Arctic Circle — far enough north that the aurora often appears right above town. You do not need to travel to the mountains: what matters is darkness, a clear sky and a little patience.

When can you see the northern lights in Piteå?

  • Season: roughly September to March. In the midnight-sun summer it is too light — the aurora is there, you just cannot see it.
  • Time of night: start looking from around 9 pm and for a few hours after. Activity often comes in waves — a quiet sky can suddenly come alive.
  • Weather matters most. Cloud is the most common reason people miss the aurora. Check the cloud forecast as carefully as the aurora forecast.

Where should you stand?

  • Away from street lights. A couple of hundred metres from lamps makes a big difference — give your eyes 10–15 minutes to adjust to the dark.
  • Look north and up. A strong display can fill the whole sky.
  • Open spaces such as fields and shorelines give a clear view of the horizon. Never walk onto ice you do not know is safe.
  • At Stations Hotellet it can appear right outside the door — the photo at the top was taken here.

Reading the forecasts

Aurora apps and websites show a Kp index (0–9) for how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is. The further north you are, the lower the Kp you need. At our latitude the aurora can show even when the Kp value is moderate — so do not give up on a clear evening just because the number is low. Combine it with a cloud forecast.

Photographing the aurora

  • Phone: the night mode on modern phones often captures more colour than the eye sees. Rest the phone on something steady.
  • Camera: tripod, manual focus at infinity, wide aperture, ISO around 1600–3200 and 5–15 second exposures. Shorter when the aurora moves fast.
  • Cold drains batteries fast — keep a spare in an inside pocket.

Dress for the wait

In midwinter it can drop to −20 °C or colder. Layers, warm boots, hat and mittens — you stand still longer than you think. A thermos of something hot makes the wait better.
